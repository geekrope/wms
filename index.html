We want to test the series for the presence of persistent trend. One approach would be to fit a linear regression to the item counts and perform a t-test: \[t = \frac{\hat{\beta_1} - 0}{\mathrm{SE}(\beta_1)} \sim t_{n - 2}\] which is t-distributed under the null hypothesis \(H_0: \beta_1 = 0\) [1] .


However the process exhibits a random walk behavior as \(Y_t = Y_{t-1} + \Delta_t\), where we assume the deltas to be i.i.d. normal with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\). We test the hypothesis \(H_0: \mu \geq 0\) against \(H_1: \mu < 0\) using the t-statistic. For this we use the decomposition \(\Delta_t=\mu + \varepsilon_t\) and fit 1-d linear regression (degenerate case of linear regression) to determine the parameter \(\mu\). The closed form solution is given by \(\hat{\mu}=\overline{\Delta}\) and the t-statistic in this case has \(n-1\) degrees of freedom \[t=\frac{\overline{\Delta}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{n - 1}\sum_{t=1}^{n} (\Delta_t - \overline{\Delta})^2} / \sqrt{n}} \sim t_{n - 1}\]


We perform both tests since the OLS method helps to assess the local behaviour and the residuals within a short time frame tend to be less correlated.
